# Draft cover letter for SIMAX

Dear Editors,

We submit the manuscript **“Nuclear-Norm Best Approximation at Rank-Deficient Matrix-Line Crossings: Certificate Geometry and Singular Smoothing”** for consideration in the *SIAM Journal on Matrix Analysis and Applications*.

The paper studies nuclear-norm best approximation from a matrix line through the geometry of its exact, tangency-constrained certificate slice. We classify feasibility and uniqueness of that slice, derive its minimum-Frobenius member by clipped singular-value water-filling, and characterize exactly when clipping is unnecessary. An admissible Fenchel family then unifies the hard and smooth constructions: the Huber regularizer has the water-filled center, while pseudo-Huber generally selects a different spectral center.

The smoothing family also reveals that the boundary exponent is a tail law. A polynomial derivative tail of order (p) gives exponent (p/(p+1)), whereas finite saturation gives a linear displacement. Pseudo-Huber is the (p=2) member and hence has the sharp two-thirds boundary law; standard Huber has the corresponding first-order law.

For arbitrary rectangular matrix lines and arbitrary corank, the paper derives analytic multiplier and returned-direction expansions at strict rank-deficient minimizers, including intrinsic computable first corrections. When compact-polar stationarity is already valid, the multiplier and direction exhibit cubic/quadratic superconvergence. We give an explicit quintic/quartic refinement after a second cancellation and identify the full odd-order multiplier hierarchy. At transverse square corank-one boundaries, a moving-crossing normal form provides a uniform cubic balance through the strict-to-boundary transition.

Finally, we prove that replacing the full certificate slice by the compact polar factor fails on a nonempty open set, even when both the scalar approximant and exact certificate are unique. This structural result explains why the complete slice and its selected centers are needed; it is presented after the certificate geometry and rate theory rather than as the paper's primary contribution.

Classical nuclear-norm duality, subdifferential formulas, Birkhoff--James orthogonality, and Schatten-limit selection are explicitly treated as background. Relative to the Watson--Ziętak best-approximation literature, the new object is the certificate face intersected with an additional tangency hyperplane, together with its Fenchel centers and singular regularization asymptotics. The selection acts on the constrained certificate side, not on the approximant side studied by Legg--Ward and related strict-approximation work.

The manuscript contains no external data. A technical supplement and a deterministic archive accompany the submission. Ten assertion-based checks reproduce the displayed constants and cover tall, wide, arbitrary-corank, rank-deficient-compression, repeated-singular-value, non-diagonal direction, smoothing-tail, multiplier-nonuniqueness, and higher-order cancellation cases.
